# Command Latency Safety Evidence

Generated by `TestCommandLatencyProfile` — go-RCP v0.71.0

**Workload duration:** 5s  
**GC pressure:** ~64 MiB/s (64 KiB allocations at ~1 ms interval)  
**Watchdog rate:** 100 Hz (one `udp.Controller.Write` per endpoint per tick, 5 endpoints)  

---

## GSN Safety Argument

**Claim (C-1):** go-RCP command delivery over the request.Dispatcher/udp.Controller request/response path meets the 5000 µs watchdog half-period latency budget under sustained GC pressure.

**Goal (G-1):** Demonstrate that Max(Send latency) < 5000 µs over a 5s workload with ~64 MiB/s GC allocation pressure, with ≥ 10 000 samples.

**Strategy (S-1):** Empirical measurement over a real loopback UDP socket (udp.Server/udp.Router serving one request.Dispatcher per endpoint, driven by udp.Controller.Write) under realistic GC load. Latency sampled for every `Write` call, end-to-end including request encode, socket round trip, server-side Dispatch (Submit+Pump+Response), and response decode. Percentiles computed over the full sample set.

**Evidence (E-1):**

### Send latency (udp.Controller.Write, 2495 samples)

| Metric | Measured | Threshold | Result |

| P50    | 77 µs   | —         | —      |
| P99    | 198 µs   | —         | —      |

**Assumptions (A-1):**

- This measures a real UDP/IP loopback socket (`127.0.0.1`), not the in-process mock the pre-TC18 evidence used — loopback network-stack overhead is included, real physical-network latency is not.
- GOMAXPROCS is at the OS default; CPU isolation (cgroups + IRQ affinity) can further reduce jitter.
- Each endpoint's request.Dispatcher uses a fresh request.Sequencer and no AccessCheck; a deployment with a configured SafeStateCheck or AccessCheck will see additional per-request overhead not captured here.

**Residual risk (R-1):**

- Real RC Servers operate over a physical Ethernet network (with or without TSN), adding non-deterministic latency beyond loopback.
- GC STW pauses of > 1 ms are possible under extreme allocation rates.
- This evidence covers the udp transport only; a future TSN or shmem transport (see ROADMAP.md) requires its own latency characterisation before integration.
